# Manage the Unmanageable

## Managing up

- Understand your boss
  - What kind of persons are they?
  - what are their hot buttons?
  - You don't have to like or admire your boss, nor do you have to hate him. You do have to manage him, however, so that he becomes your resource for achievement, accomplishment and personal success.
  - think about communication with your boss. one and ones?
  - what are the initiatives my boss is responsible for?
  - how can I contribute to his success 
  - I need to access his ego: I need to understand his ego
- Package your communication
  - your boss need to know that you are taking care of stuff without distracting him
  - they need to know of things you rugalarly handle with but not the details
  - initially you should report on everything till you understand your responsibilities and job split with your boss
  - keep reports to one or two pages (professional)
  - report regularly without exception -> very important for gaining confidence of your boss
- Understand your boss's boss
  - how can you help your boss be successful
  - understand what your boss is being judged on by your boss's boss
  - what are his objectives
    - improving quality
    - on-time delivery
    - innovation
    - something else about products
    - Issues reporting
    - Collaboration and partnering
    - revenue and/or expenses
  - ask how you can help
- Timing
  - When does your boss want to hear from you?
  - timing is important: never underestimate the value of good timing 
  - know when important company meetings take place or decision are made
  - be a model employee
   - be low maintenance -> do not bring every problem to your boss for a solution
  - pick your battles carefully and take only important issues to your boss for resolution
  - always bring possible solutions and do your homework -> than seek for counsel
  - always bring three solutions 
  - underpromise, overdeliver
  - provide more than what is expected

## Managing Yourself

P.178ff

- Personal style
  - balance between appearing like just one of your team and dressing to impress upper management 
  - know your staff: anniversary, how many children, name of spouse, hometown, hobbies, vacations
- Time and priority management
  - do not tolerate poor meeting etiquette in others
  - every hour of planning saves about a day of wasted time and effort 
  - distinguish between urgent and important tasks
- Communications management
  - control the flood of information - you cannot let it control you 
  - see email etiquette 
  - slots for checking mails (start & end of the day)
  - pick your attendance in meetings carefully and consciously
    - don't accept because you were invited
    - do I really need to be there?
- Management practices
  - to be an effective manager you must be an effective communicator. The most effective communicators are good listeners
  - Wisdom is the reward you get for a lifetime of listening when you're rather have been talking -Aristotle
  - pay attention to the person
  - listen reflectively (active listening)
  - make sure that you are truly working on important things that will make a major difference in the long run
  - make progress every day 
- Follow-up management
  - follow up on tasks and assignments 
- Find a mentor

### E-mail etiquette 

Is e-mail the right vehicle?

. Phone or face-to-face may be more appropriate. Be clear and to the point.

• Create a meaningful subject line. Use the best possible descrip- tion of your topic and purpose in the subject line. Topics change in e-mail threads-update the subject line appropriately!

. Clearly indicate when an action or decision is needed, if comments are requested, or if the message is just an FYL

Use To/CC/BCC appropriately.

People on the To line should expect an action item: the CC line is

for FYI only. Be very careful when using BCC. Forwarding a person a sent e-mail may be safer.

Avoid overusing Reply All.

If the message was sent to a distribution list, consider whether everyone on the list really needs to see your reply,

• Reduce threads to just the appropriate people, distribution lists are not always appropriate.

Stay on topic.

Make sure your comments are appropriate for the subject-line

topic.

• Stick to one topic in a thread. • If the topic changes, start a new thread with a new subject.

Reduce lengthy e-mail threads.

If an e-mail thread goes back and forth more than three times, call or set up a meeting to discuss the topic.

⚫ Be careful when forwarding threads to new or external people: there may be something in the thread you do not want everyone

to see.
