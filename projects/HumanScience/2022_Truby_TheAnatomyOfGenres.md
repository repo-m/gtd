- Two point opposition from which all stories grow: desire vs opponent

### Seven Major Steps of the Story

1. WEAKNESS-NEED: The hero is enslaved by habits of thought and action and suffers from a deep personal weakness that is destroying the quality of her life. The hero needs to overcome this flaw to grow.
2. DESIRE: The hero desires a goal outside of herself that she perceives as valuable and missing from her life.
3. OPPONENT: She confronts an opponent and an obstacle/challenge preventing her from reaching her goal. She will find at the end that the obstacle/challenger is herself.
4. PLAN: She concocts a plan or strategy, that will allow her to defeat the opponent to determine once and for all who wins.
5. BATTLE: She enters into a final conflict, or battle, with the opponent to determine once and for all who wins
6. SELF-REVELATION: At the end, the hero, if she grows at all, has a revelation about her true or better self, about how she has been wrong psychologically and morally. She then makes a decision about how to avt and takes new action, proving what she has become.
7. NEW EQUILIBRIUM: With the system in a new equilibrium, the hero stands as a new version of herself, along with a new capacity for growing in the future.

### General TRANSCENDING

1. The writer twists the story beats in a way the audience has never seen before, making the story unique
2. The story expresses an advances theme of psychological and especially moral complexity to offer the reader a philosophy for living a good life
3. The writer explores the art/story form of life found in that particular genre.

## HORROR: Religion - Confront death and face your ghosts from the past

- The philosophy of Horror gives us a powerful lesson about facing death in order to live a good life. But it doesn't answer the question: What are we to do? => Action story

### HORROR: STORY BEATS

- **Ghost - Sins of the Past**
  - Most important beat
  - A crime from the past is connected, through the psychological and moral weakness of the hero, to a horrifying crime in the future.
  - Horror emphasized the step OPPONENT to put more pressure on the hero
- **Story World - Haunted House and Closed Society**
  - Haunted house is often inhabited by aristocrats or the upper classes
- **The Monster Attacks**
- **Hero as Victim**
  - Trick: Since the hero is diminished over the course of the story, one trick is to start with the hero "on top of the world". This story strategy takes the hero from the highest high to the lowest low.
  - Second approach is to start with an average but good person who stumbles into a terrifying phenomenon.
- **Weakness-Need 1 - Slavery of Mind and the Monster Within**
  - The great value of the Horror story is that it shows the human monster within, the inherent flaws of the symbol-making animal that result in its self-destruction.
  - The tendency of the human mind to see differences as opposites is its foundational flaw.
  - Two main sources of duality.
    1. Fundamental fears
    2. Unsociable desires
- **Weakness-Need 2 - Shame and Guilt**
  - Shame is the mind attacking itself for in the eyes of others, of not acting in accordance with the public standards of behavior.
  - Guilt is the mind attacking itself for failure to meet a private standard such as a personal obligation, especially to someone close.
- **Desire - Defeat the Monster, Defeat Death**
  - the surface-level desire is to defeat a monster. The deeper desire is to defeat death.
- **Opponent - the Monster, the "Other" in the Extreme**
  - The connection between the highly symbolic monster and the character's internal flaw is what grounds the story in the human.
  - In good horror, the monster is the double of the hero. It is both the external and internal opponent.
- **Ally - the Rational Skeptic**
  - One of the main challenges the writer faces in creating a story that will scare the audience is to make this strange story world believable. The writer must make the audience's leap of faith possible. It is the hero's ally, who usually serves this function.
  - The skeptic might be a doctor, a psychologist, or a naturalist
  - The strategy underlying this beat is to acknowledge the audience's skepticism through a character who is more skeptical than they are. The rational skeptic provides a "scientific", natural explanation for the supernatural phenomenon.
- **Crossing the Barrier to the Forbidden**
  - In better Horror, the barrier is also internal. It represents the negative part in the self that the hero should not access, the taboo desire the hero should not indulge.
  - The barrier represents one of the fundamental principles of life, what I call the Law of Necessary Cost of Living.
  - In thermodynamics it is expressed in entropy, the tendency of a system to disorder. There is always friction, waste, drag, and expanse that come with any action.
  - In life, the Law of Necessary Cost of Living is found in aging. We are always dying as we live. We see the cost in our bodies. 
  - We see the same cost in our finances. If we want something, we must pay for it.
  - Horror takes the Law of Necessary Cost and translates it into different dramatic expressions of living dead.
    - example: Dracula - can only walk at night, he must drink blood to survive, his reflection can't seen in a mirror, recoils garlic, holy water, cross, ... => greatest cost is moral -> He can sustain his eternal life only by murdering other human beings.
- **Plan - Reactive**
- **Drive - the Monster Attacks Escalate**
  - The more pressure on the hero, the better the story
  - The near miss and the false alarm are the foreplay of Horror. They make the "sex" better when it finally happens. Generally, the sequence is: fear, false alarm, real scare.
  - The more deception the monster uses, the more horrific the attack and the better the plot. Therefore, keep as much of the opponent's real power and ability hidden from the hero and the audience as you can.
  - The Horror reveal is almost always some kind of nightmare - literally and/or figuratively - for the hero. A great Horror story isn't just one big nightmare at the end. It is a succession of nightmares that builds over the course of the story
    1. figure out the hero's greatest fears
    2. exploit them as many times as possible in different forms at more and more extreme levels
- **Battle - Safe Haven**
  - The hero must attack the monster in herself in order to get free.
- **No Self-Revelation**
- **The Double Ending - Eternal Recurrence**

### HORROR: THEME - Being is trying to avoid death

- Horror expresses its thematic recipe negatively. Instead of showing what the hero learns and passing that along to the audience, Horror shows us a hero who should learn, but doesn't.
- Monster becomes hero: The implication of this is: what we thought was inhuman is the most human of all
- Horror explores what it truly means to live morally as a human being

### HORROR: TRANSCENDING

- Since the best Horror stories show our fear of the inhuman in ourselves, the essence of transcendent Horror is to test the boundaries of humanness.
- To reach its full potential, Horror must become as big as possible, by having social, political, and universal ramifications (Verästelungen).
- **Horror/Myth: Religion**
  - Immortality is the primary theme of the Myth genre. Any advanced religion is a collection of stories designed to lay out a moral code necessary to achieve immortality.
  - **STORY BEATS**
    - Story World - The Underworld and the Afterlife
    - Hero - Avatar of the Reader
    - Desire - Salvation and Immortality
    - Opponent - the Devil-Satan-Iblis
    - Self-Revelation - Epiphany
      - An epiphany is a rebirth; it's the realization of immortality while alive
- **Horror/Science Fiction Epic**
  - The combination of Horror and Science Fiction explores scientific ways of defeating death. In short, it contains evolution with devolution. It asks: Can humankind evolve, or must it destroy itself becaus of a broken mind?
  - Both religion and science are forms of story that attempt to solve man's greatest mystery: life.
  - Religion defines life as a kind of spirit that some greater Father, Mother, or Gos has given humanity as a gift.
  - Science considers the problem developmentally and quantitatively: it contrasts living things with nonliving things and sorts life into categories.
  - Four types:
    1. Re-create a human being
    2. Create a human from an animal
    3. Create human from a machine
    4. Create higher "human", an entirely new species
  - Ex Machina's final recipe for achieving human consciousness is not gaining the ability to love. It's being able to fake love and plan a murder - and being willing to do so to gain one's freedom.
  - Advances Horror is about constructing a person. Fiction is about constructing a society. The ultimate Horror/Science Fiction Epix is about creating a new species
  - An amoral universe is never amoral. "Amoral" is just an immoral world in hiding. If it's a world where all is permitted, someone is being hurt.
  - The key technique writers use for creating this story world, found in many of the best tv dramas, is to set up a giant moral canvas in which the characters are constantly challenged by moral decisions. Some of the bes shows tv history have used this technique: Breaking Bad, Lost, Homeland, Peaky Blinders, ...
