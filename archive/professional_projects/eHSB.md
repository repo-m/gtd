# eHSB

- Project: eHSB (Electronic Main Fuse Box) – 800 A Next-Gen Power Distribution
- Role: Test Manager for ECU and Function (OEM Side)
- Scale: 6-company consortium (OEMs, Tier-1s, Service Partners) including HIL, pre-series, and fleet vehicle integration.
- Core Challenge: Establishing a unified validation framework and high-power (800 A) safety-critical (ASIL C) infrastructure from scratch where no processes or test benches existed.

## The Challenge - Why?

- Safety Complexity: Managing 800 A loads required rigorous isolation (FFI) and thermal safety logic (ASIL C) to prevent catastrophic failures like thermal runaway or logic resets during voltage sags.
- Process Vacuum: The involvement of six different organizations without a centralized test strategy or shared infrastructure created high risks for traceability gaps and integration delays.
- Infrastructure Deficit: Lack of pre-existing HIL systems or test vehicles for high-current applications meant the entire validation environment had to be designed and sourced concurrently with ECU development.

- Derating Curves:
    - Thermal Protection: Implemented dynamic current limits based on MOSFET junction temperatures to prevent hardware damage.
    - Current Scaling: Logic that reduces the maximum allowable current (e.g., from 100% to 70%) as ambient or component temperature rises.
    - Availability vs. Protection: Designed to maintain reduced functionality rather than an immediate hard shutdown, ensuring the system stays operational as long as safe.
- Safe State:
    - Fail-Safe (OFF): Non-essential or comfort loads default to "Open" (OFF) during a critical failure to isolate the fault.
    - Fail-Operational (ON): Safety-critical clusters (Steering/Braking) utilize hardware-defined "Default ON" or redundant paths to ensure power persists even during MCU failure.
    - FTTI Compliance: Verified that the transition to the Safe State occurs within the defined Fault Tolerant Time Interval (typically <10 ms for high-power transients).
- Diagnostic Coverage:
    - Latent Fault Detection: Power-On Self-Tests (BIST) verify that eFuses are not "stuck-on" before the vehicle begins its mission.
    - Plausibility Checks: Cross-referencing total input current against the sum of all branch currents to detect sensor drift or leakage.
    - ASIL C Requirements: High-accuracy sensing ( error) for overcurrent and undervoltage to ensure high diagnostic coverage () for the power distribution logic.

## Technical Solution

- Proof of Concept (PoC) Vehicle:
    - Successfully built a PoC Vehicle to demonstrate the integrated eHBS performance.
    - Used this vehicle as the final validation stage to verify power prioritization and the transition to a "Minimal Risk Maneuver" (MRM) under real-world current profiles.
- Executed requirements-based verification on a Component-HIL, achieving 100% coverage of functional and safety specifications.
  - VDA 450 Fail-Operational Logic: Implemented load shedding of comfort functions to prioritize power for safety-critical systems (e.g., steering) during faults, ensuring continuous availability.

### HIL-Tests (Component-Level)

- Focus
  - Precision Load Control: Used Sink/Source devices for 800 A regulation to simulate both high-power consumption and energy recuperation.
  - Safe Fault Injection: Integrated a switchboard to trigger repeatable "hard" short circuits and open loads without damaging hardware.
  - Timing Validation: Verified safety-critical 1–4 ms separation windows for ASIL C clusters under controlled transients.
  - Logic Verification: Validated fault debouncing algorithms to distinguish between normal inrush currents and genuine failures. +Fuse-Blow +Precharge
- Blind Spots: Real Environment
  - EMI/EMC Noise: Unable to replicate the specific electromagnetic interference generated by neighboring high-voltage components.
  - Thermal Cross-talk: Missing the localized heat soak effects from the vehicle's engine bay or battery compartment packaging.
- Trade-offs: Controllability vs. Physics: Gained high electrical fidelity and automation at the cost of excluding physical stressors like harness impedance and vibration.

### Vehicle Tests (System-Level)

- Focus:
  - VDA 450 Validation: Verified functional prioritization and load-shedding logic within the full Power Distribution Network (EBN).
  - System Availability: Confirmed continuous power for steering and braking during induced faults by shedding comfort loads.
  - MRM Execution: Proven transition to a Minimal Risk Maneuver (MRM) under real-world current profiles and thermal conditions.
  - PoC Integration: Demonstrated eHSB performance in a physical Proof of Concept vehicle to secure series production readiness. +Atlas; Unplug Fuses & see system response
- Blind Spots:
  - Destructive Testing: Extreme edge-case faults (e.g., massive over-voltage spikes) are avoided to prevent catastrophic damage to prototypes.
  - Repeatability: Hard to reproduce exact environmental conditions (temperature/SOC) for consistent back-to-back comparisons.
- Trade-offs: Complexity vs. Risk: Gained maximum system-level realism but faced limited repeatability and high safety risks during 800 A short-circuit testing.
