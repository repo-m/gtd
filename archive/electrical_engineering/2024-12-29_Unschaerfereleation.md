# 2024-12-29 Unschärferelation

You can observe animals with a telescope without interacting with them. This is because photons from a light source (i.e. the sun) hit the animal, then go to your eye. No interaction between you and the animal. When "observing" sub-atomic particles it's completely different. You have to throw other particles at them that have a relatively high mass, thus affecting the position of the thing being observed. So building a model is difficult for this reason and because the returned particle still doesn't describe the thing you hit accurately any way. So you have to throw particles multiple times and build probabilty models. "observing" and "interacting" in quantum mechanics has a very different meaning than when applied to much more massive objects.

Observed isn't the right word interact is better. But the thing is there is no observation without interaction.
